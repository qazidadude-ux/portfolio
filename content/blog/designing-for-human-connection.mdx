Every interface is a conversation, whether it's designed like one or not. The ones that feel best are the ones that respond like a person would.

## Tone lives in more than copy

Micro-interactions, transition timing, and error states all carry tone, the same way copy does. A form that shakes angrily at a typo says something different than one that gently highlights the field.

## Design for the bad day, not just the good one

Most usability testing happens with calm, patient users. Real users are often tired, frustrated, or in a hurry. Design your empty states, errors, and edge cases for that person.

## Familiarity is a feature

Novel interaction patterns can feel delightful in a demo and confusing in daily use. Human connection often comes from an interface behaving exactly the way someone expects it to.
